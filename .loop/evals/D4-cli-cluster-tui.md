eval: D4-cli-cluster-tui

**Definition of Done:** a `localstack monitor` Textual TUI showing exactly four
things — Vault seal state, Nomad node status, per-job allocation health, and
Consul critical checks — refreshing without ever blocking on a dead source,
with every degraded state named, rendered and snapshot-tested. Auth comes
entirely from D2, the read-capable Nomad role from F7, and every address from
D1's `config.py`.

**The scope constraint is the operator's own framing:** "not a Grafana
replacement but a panel for developers". Grafana, Prometheus and Loki already
run here. The Grafana-boundary guardrail below makes that checkable rather
than aspirational, because the natural drift of a status panel is toward a
dashboard.

**Revised 2026-07-31 after the plan review found the health model false.**
`JobSummary.Failed` and `.Lost` are cumulative lifetime allocation counters,
not current state: measured live, 8 of the 14 healthy `service` jobs carry
`Failed > 0` (`memex` 31, `phoenix` 15 with `Lost: 2`, `haproxy` 8) while
running at their full desired count. The previous rows could not fail on that,
because the fixtures were authored from the same false model. Four rows are
new: **3** (the loud-counter jobs render healthy, and the counters cannot
reach the panel), **5** (one call per refresh, which is also what makes the
load claim true), **10** (the brokered read token actually works) and **13**
(no hardcoded address, from N4). Row 4 is rewritten around
`/v1/jobs/statuses`, and row 11 now serves a fast first response so a last
known value exists to keep. Rows 9 and 10 split
the 403 question so the marker can no longer score 100% on a panel that
renders "denied" in both headline widgets. Rows 2, 12, 13, 17 and 18 guard
their greps with `test -d` against D1's real package path, so they fail when
it is absent instead of passing vacuously on a path that does not exist. Row
13 is new, from N4.

**The trap.** A TUI is judged by how it looks when things are fine, and used
when they are not. The degraded-state rows cover what a developer actually
opens it during. Rows 3 and 4 cover the quieter failure: healthy jobs reported
as broken, which on this cluster would be 11 of 19 rows on day one.
